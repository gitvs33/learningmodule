Module 3: Positional Encoding

Self-attention has no inherent sense of word order. Positional Encodings are special vectors added to each word's embedding to give the model a "timestamp" for its position.

Why Is This Needed?

The attention mechanism sees the input as a "bag of words" — it knows which words are present, but not their order. Without positional information, the sentences "The cat chased the dog" and "The dog chased the cat" would look nearly identical to the model.

To fix this, we inject a unique vector, the Positional Encoding, for each position. This vector is added to the word's own meaning (its embedding), creating a final representation that contains information about both what the word is and where it is.

The Formula

The model uses sine and cosine functions at different frequencies to generate these unique vectors:

PE(pos, 2i) = sin(pos / 100002i/d)

PE(pos, 2i+1) = cos(pos / 100002i/d)

Decoding the Heatmap: Colors and Waves

Each cell's color represents a value between -1 and 1, generated by sine/cosine waves:

  • Bright Red = Value is close to **+1** (the peak of the wave).
  • White = Value is close to **0** (the middle of the wave).
  • Bright Blue = Value is close to **-1** (the trough of the wave).

The columns on the left are "fast waves" (high frequency), alternating color quickly. This gives a precise signal for close-by words.

The columns on the right are "slow waves" (low frequency), forming broad bands. This gives a general signal for a word's overall location.
