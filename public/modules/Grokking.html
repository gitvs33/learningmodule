Grokking

Sometimes, a model generalizes long after it has overfit the training data. This delayed "Aha!" moment is called grokking.

What's Happening Here?

Notice the two phases of learning. First, the Training Loss drops quickly. This is the model simply **memorizing** the answers for the data it has seen. However, its Generalization Loss** remains high—it's useless on new, unseen data.

Then, after a long period of seemingly no progress, the model suddenly **"groks"** the underlying pattern. The Generalization Loss plummets. It has transitioned from brute-force memorization to genuine understanding. Why this happens is still an active area of research, but it suggests that simpler, more general solutions might be "harder to find" for the network, requiring more optimization time to discover.
